# Glossary

A working glossary of important Sanskrit technical terms used throughout the Indian Philosophy curriculum.

The glossary is intended as a **study reference**, not as a substitute for the detailed treatment of each term in the relevant texts. Technical meanings can vary between traditions and contexts; where necessary, the same term should therefore be read in relation to the philosophical school under discussion.

---

## How to Use This Glossary

For each term, learn:

1. **Sanskrit form**
2. **IAST transliteration**
3. **Basic meaning**
4. **Technical philosophical meaning**
5. **Tradition or context**
6. **Relationship to other terms**

Do not assume that an English translation captures the complete technical meaning of a Sanskrit term.

---

# A

### Abhāva — अभाव

**Basic meaning:** Absence, non-existence.

**Technical use:** A category concerned with absence or negation. In the Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika tradition, *abhāva* receives systematic ontological and epistemological treatment.

---

### Adṛṣṭa — अदृष्ट

**Basic meaning:** Unseen, unperceived.

**Technical use:** An unseen factor invoked in discussions of action, causal continuity, merit, demerit, and consequences.

---

### Ātman — आत्मन्

**Basic meaning:** Self.

**Technical use:** The self or enduring subject, particularly in Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika discussions of cognition, desire, pleasure, pain, agency, memory, and liberation.

---

### Āpta — आप्त

**Basic meaning:** Trustworthy, reliable person.

**Technical use:** A reliable speaker whose testimony can serve as a source of valid verbal cognition in Nyāya.

---

# A–K

### Anubhava — अनुभव

**Basic meaning:** Experience, presentative cognition.

**Technical use:** Cognition that is not merely recollective; contrasted with *smṛti* in relevant epistemological discussions.

---

### Anumāna — अनुमान

**Basic meaning:** Inference.

**Technical use:** Cognition produced through an appropriate inferential relation, especially through a known reason and its established *vyāpti*.

**Related terms:** *hetu, sādhya, pakṣa, vyāpti*.

---

### Anumiti — अनुमिति

**Basic meaning:** Inferential cognition.

**Technical use:** The cognition produced by inference.

---

### Anumāna-pramāṇa — अनुमानप्रमाण

**Basic meaning:** Inference as a means of knowledge.

**Technical use:** The inferential means by which valid inferential cognition is produced.

---

### Apūrva — अपूर्व

**Basic meaning:** Previously non-existent; unprecedented.

**Technical use:** In Mīmāṃsā, a technical concept associated with the relation between prescribed action and its later result.

---

### Arthāpatti — अर्थापत्ति

**Basic meaning:** Postulation, presumption.

**Technical use:** A proposed means of knowledge in some Indian philosophical traditions, involving the postulation of what must be the case to account for an otherwise unexplained fact.

**Note:** Its status as an independent *pramāṇa* varies among traditions.

---

### Avacchedaka — अवच्छेदक

**Basic meaning:** Limiter, delimitor.

**Technical use:** A technical term especially important in Navya-Nyāya analysis for specifying the limiting condition under which a relation, property, or cognition is being analyzed.

---

### Avacchinna — अवच्छिन्न

**Basic meaning:** Limited, delimited.

**Technical use:** Something characterized or restricted by a particular limiting condition.

---

### Avyabhicāra — अव्यभिचार

**Basic meaning:** Non-deviation, non-violation.

**Technical use:** The absence of deviation in a relation; particularly relevant to the reliability of an inferential reason and *vyāpti*.

---

# B

### Bādha — बाध

**Basic meaning:** Sublation, cancellation, defeat.

**Technical use:** The sublation or defeat of a previously cognized object or proposition by a later cognition.

---

### Buddhi — बुद्धि

**Basic meaning:** Intellect, cognition, understanding.

**Technical use:** Its meaning varies by tradition. In Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika contexts it can refer to cognition as a quality of the self.

---

# D

### Darśana — दर्शन

**Basic meaning:** Seeing, vision, viewpoint.

**Technical use:** A philosophical system or disciplined mode of philosophical investigation.

---

### Dravya — द्रव्य

**Basic meaning:** Substance, entity.

**Technical use:** One of the principal ontological categories of Vaiśeṣika and Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika thought.

---

### Dharma — धर्म

**Basic meaning:** Duty, law, right conduct, sustaining principle.

**Technical use:** A central concept of Mīmāṃsā concerning what is to be done and, in broader traditions, a principle of right conduct or order.

---

### Dṛṣṭānta — दृष्टान्त

**Basic meaning:** Example, illustrative instance.

**Technical use:** An example used in argument, especially in the Nyāya five-membered reasoning structure.

---

# G

### Guṇa — गुण

**Basic meaning:** Quality, attribute.

**Technical use:** An ontological category in Vaiśeṣika. Qualities are distinguished from substances and actions.

---

# H

### Hetu — हेतु

**Basic meaning:** Reason, cause, ground.

**Technical use:** The reason or probans used in an inference.

**Related terms:** *pakṣa, sādhya, vyāpti, hetvābhāsa*.

---

### Hetvābhāsa — हेत्वाभास

**Basic meaning:** Fallacious reason; appearance of a reason.

**Technical use:** A defective reason that appears to establish a conclusion but fails to do so.

---

# J

### Jñāna — ज्ञान

**Basic meaning:** Cognition, knowledge.

**Technical use:** A broad term for cognition or awareness; its precise philosophical interpretation depends on the tradition.

---

# K

### Karma — कर्म

**Basic meaning:** Action.

**Technical use:** In Vaiśeṣika, an ontological category referring to action or motion. In broader Indian traditions, the term can also refer to action and its consequences.

---

### Khaṇḍa — खण्ड

**Basic meaning:** Section, part.

**Technical use:** A major division of a philosophical work, such as the *Tattvacintāmaṇi-khaṇḍas*.

---

### Kṣaṇa — क्षण

**Basic meaning:** Moment.

**Technical use:** A temporal unit or moment; its philosophical significance differs across traditions.

---

# M

### Manas — मनस्

**Basic meaning:** Mind.

**Technical use:** In Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika, a subtle internal organ or substance involved in cognition and the connection between the self and the senses.

---

### Mīmāṃsā — मीमांसा

**Basic meaning:** Investigation, inquiry.

**Technical use:** A major Indian philosophical tradition, especially concerned with dharma, Vedic authority, interpretation, language, and epistemology.

---

### Mithyājñāna — मिथ्याज्ञान

**Basic meaning:** Erroneous cognition.

**Technical use:** Cognition that fails to correspond appropriately to its object.

---

### Mokṣa — मोक्ष

**Basic meaning:** Liberation.

**Technical use:** Release from the relevant form of bondage and suffering. Its precise nature varies between traditions.

---

# N

### Nigamana — निगमन

**Basic meaning:** Conclusion.

**Technical use:** The concluding member of the classical Nyāya five-membered argument.

---

### Nigrahasthāna — निग्रहस्थान

**Basic meaning:** Ground of defeat.

**Technical use:** A recognized defect or failure in debate that constitutes a ground for defeat.

---

### Nirṇaya — निर्णय

**Basic meaning:** Determination, ascertainment.

**Technical use:** Determinate judgment or settled cognition following the removal of doubt through appropriate reasoning.

---

### Nirvikalpaka — निर्विकल्पक

**Basic meaning:** Without conceptual determination.

**Technical use:** A technical term in discussions of perception concerning cognition lacking certain forms of conceptual determination.

---

# P

### Pakṣa — पक्ष

**Basic meaning:** Side, subject, thesis.

**Technical use:** In inference, the subject in which the *sādhya* is to be established.

---

### Padārtha — पदार्थ

**Basic meaning:** Object, category, entity.

**Technical use:** A fundamental category or object of philosophical analysis, especially in Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika.

---

### Pramā — प्रमा

**Basic meaning:** Valid cognition, true cognition.

**Technical use:** Cognition regarded as valid or knowledge-producing.

---

### Pramāṇa — प्रमाण

**Basic meaning:** Means of valid knowledge.

**Technical use:** A reliable means by which *pramā* is produced.

---

### Prameya — प्रमेय

**Basic meaning:** Object of knowledge.

**Technical use:** That which is to be known or established through valid cognition.

---

### Prāmāṇya — प्रामाण्य

**Basic meaning:** Validity, authoritativeness.

**Technical use:** The property or status of a cognition by virtue of which it is considered valid or knowledge-producing.

---

### Pratijñā — प्रतिज्ञा

**Basic meaning:** Proposition, thesis.

**Technical use:** The thesis or proposition to be established in a Nyāya argument.

---

### Pratyakṣa — प्रत्यक्ष

**Basic meaning:** Perception; directly before the senses.

**Technical use:** A means or form of valid cognition arising through appropriate perceptual conditions.

---

### Pratyabhijñā — प्रत्यभिज्ञा

**Basic meaning:** Recognition.

**Technical use:** Cognition in which something previously encountered is recognized as the same or as connected with what was previously known.

---

### Pratipakṣa — प्रतिपक्ष

**Basic meaning:** Opposing position.

**Technical use:** A contrary or rival thesis in philosophical argument.

---

### Prayojana — प्रयोजन

**Basic meaning:** Purpose, objective, motive.

**Technical use:** The purpose or end motivating inquiry or action.

---

# S

### Sādhana — साधन

**Basic meaning:** Means, instrument, method.

**Technical use:** A means employed for accomplishing a goal or establishing a conclusion.

---

### Sādhya — साध्य

**Basic meaning:** What is to be established.

**Technical use:** The property or conclusion to be established through inference.

---

### Samavāya — समवाय

**Basic meaning:** Inherence.

**Technical use:** A distinctive relation in Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika ontology, used to account for relations such as whole and parts, substance and qualities, and universal and instances.

---

### Sāmānya — सामान्य

**Basic meaning:** Generality, universal.

**Technical use:** A universal or common feature present in multiple instances.

---

### Saṃśaya — संशय

**Basic meaning:** Doubt.

**Technical use:** A cognition involving uncertainty between alternative possibilities.

---

### Śabda — शब्द

**Basic meaning:** Word, sound, verbal expression.

**Technical use:** Depending on context, may refer to linguistic expression, verbal testimony, or the source of verbal cognition.

---

### Śabdapramāṇa — शब्दप्रमाण

**Basic meaning:** Verbal testimony as a means of knowledge.

**Technical use:** Valid knowledge produced through trustworthy verbal communication.

---

### Śakti — शक्ति

**Basic meaning:** Power, capacity.

**Technical use:** The semantic capacity of a word to convey its meaning in relevant theories of language.

---

### Sphoṭa — स्फोट

**Basic meaning:** A bursting forth or manifestation.

**Technical use:** A central concept in Bhartṛhari's philosophy of language concerning the manifestation or unity of linguistic meaning.

---

### Smṛti — स्मृति

**Basic meaning:** Memory, recollection.

**Technical use:** Recollection of something previously experienced or cognized.

---

### Sapakṣa — सपक्ष

**Basic meaning:** Similar case.

**Technical use:** An instance in which the property to be established is known to occur; used in the analysis of inference.

---

### Vipakṣa — विपक्ष

**Basic meaning:** Dissimilar case.

**Technical use:** An instance where the property to be established is absent; important in testing an inferential reason.

---

# T

### Tarka — तर्क

**Basic meaning:** Reasoning, hypothetical reasoning.

**Technical use:** A form of reasoning used to expose the consequences of an assumption and assist in determining the correct position. Its precise status differs from that of an independent *pramāṇa*.

---

### Upādhi — उपाधि

**Basic meaning:** Limiting condition, qualifier.

**Technical use:** A condition introduced in analyses of inferential relations, particularly where an apparent universal relation fails under closer examination.

---

### Upamāna — उपमान

**Basic meaning:** Comparison, analogy.

**Technical use:** A means of knowledge involving comparison or similarity, treated as an independent *pramāṇa* by Nyāya.

---

### Upanaya — उपनय

**Basic meaning:** Application.

**Technical use:** The application of the general relation stated in an example to the particular subject in the Nyāya five-membered argument.

---

# V

### Vāda — वाद

**Basic meaning:** Discussion, argument, debate.

**Technical use:** A form of philosophical debate oriented toward establishing what is correct through legitimate reasoning.

---

### Vākya — वाक्य

**Basic meaning:** Sentence, utterance.

**Technical use:** A linguistic unit capable of producing sentence-level cognition; its analysis varies among Nyāya, Mīmāṃsā, and Vyākaraṇa traditions.

---

### Vākyapadīya — वाक्यपदीय

**Basic meaning:** A work concerning words and sentences.

**Technical use:** Bhartṛhari's major philosophical work on language, meaning, cognition, and related questions.

---

### Vastu — वस्तु

**Basic meaning:** Thing, entity, reality.

**Technical use:** A broad term whose precise significance depends on philosophical context.

---

### Viśeṣa — विशेष

**Basic meaning:** Particularity, specificity.

**Technical use:** A fundamental ontological category in Vaiśeṣika, particularly associated with ultimate particularity.

---

### Vyāpti — व्याप्ति

**Basic meaning:** Pervasion, invariable concomitance.

**Technical use:** The necessary or invariable relation between the inferential reason and what it establishes.

**Example:**

```text
Wherever there is smoke, there is fire.
```

The inferential force depends upon an established relation between smoke and fire.

---

### Vyabhicāra — व्यभिचार

**Basic meaning:** Deviation, irregularity.

**Technical use:** Failure of a proposed inferential relation to remain invariant.

---

### Vitaṇḍā — वितण्डा

**Basic meaning:** Destructive criticism.

**Technical use:** A form of debate focused on refuting an opponent without establishing an independent counter-thesis.

---

# Y

### Yoga — योग

**Basic meaning:** Union, discipline, practice.

**Technical use:** The meaning varies considerably across traditions. In this curriculum, the term should be interpreted according to the particular philosophical context rather than given a single universal definition.

# Important Caution

Sanskrit philosophical terminology is often **school-specific**.

A term may:

- have a general meaning in Sanskrit;
- acquire a technical meaning in one school;
- receive a different technical meaning in another;
- change meaning across historical periods;
- have no exact equivalent in English.

Therefore:

> **Never assume that translating a Sanskrit term automatically explains its philosophical meaning.**

When studying a term in depth, always consult its use in the relevant primary or scholarly source.
