# Milestones

This document defines the major milestones for progressing through the Indian Philosophy curriculum.

A milestone represents a **demonstrated level of understanding and ability**, not merely the completion of a number of pages or books.

The purpose is to prevent passive reading. Progress should be measured by the ability to explain concepts, reconstruct arguments, compare positions, identify weaknesses, and reason independently.

---

# How to Use This File

For each milestone, evaluate yourself against four dimensions:

- **Knowledge** — Do I know the terminology and doctrines?
- **Understanding** — Can I explain why the doctrine is formulated?
- **Analysis** — Can I reconstruct and evaluate the arguments?
- **Independence** — Can I use the method without being guided?

A milestone is complete when the required abilities can be demonstrated consistently.

---

# Milestone 1 — Philosophical Orientation

## Corresponds to

**Level 1 — Foundations**

## You should understand

- What *darśana* means in philosophical context.
- The difference between a claim and an argument.
- The distinction between cognition, knowledge, doubt, memory, and error.
- The basic concepts of *pramā*, *pramāṇa*, and *prameya*.
- Why philosophical positions require justification.

## You should be able to

- Identify the central question in a philosophical passage.
- Distinguish premises from conclusions.
- Explain a simple philosophical argument in your own words.
- Identify an assumption underlying an argument.

## Demonstration

Take a philosophical claim and write:

```text
Question
↓
Claim
↓
Reason
↓
Supporting assumptions
↓
Conclusion
```

# Milestone 2 — Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika Categories

## Corresponds to

**Level 1 — Foundations / Level 6 — Ontology**

## You should understand

- Padārtha
- Dravya
- Guṇa
- Karma
- Sāmānya
- Viśeṣa
- Samavāya
- Abhāva

## You should be able to

- Define each category.
- Give appropriate examples.
- Distinguish neighboring categories.
- Explain why the category is philosophically necessary.
- Identify problems that arise from the proposed classification.

## Demonstration

Choose five ordinary objects and analyze them using the relevant categories.

Do not merely list categories. Explain the philosophical reasoning behind the classification.

# Milestone 3 — Understanding Pramāṇa

## Corresponds to

**Level 2 — Epistemology**

## You should understand

- Pramā
- Pramāṇa
- Prameya
- Prāmāṇya
- Valid cognition
- Error
- Doubt
- Memory

## You should be able to

- Explain the relationship between cognition and knowledge.
- Explain why a means of knowledge must be distinguished from its object.
- Distinguish valid cognition from memory and doubt.
- Explain the basic Nyāya conception of epistemic validity.

## Demonstration

Given a particular cognition, determine:

```text
What is cognized?
        ↓
What kind of cognition is it?
        ↓
What produced it?
        ↓
Is it valid?
        ↓
Why?
```

# Milestone 4 — Pratyakṣa

## Corresponds to

**Level 2 — Pratyakṣa**

## You should understand

- The Nyāya account of perception.
- The relevant conditions of perceptual cognition.
- The role of sense-object relations.
- The distinction between valid perception and error.
- Major technical distinctions in the *Pratyakṣa-khaṇḍa*.

## You should be able to

- Analyze an example of perceptual cognition.
- Identify the object, cognition, and relevant conditions.
- Explain why the cognition qualifies or fails to qualify as valid perception.
- Follow the basic structure of a technical perceptual argument.

## Demonstration

Analyze a perceptual case without immediately appealing to modern psychological explanations.

Begin with the Nyāya framework itself.

# Milestone 5 — Anumāna

## Corresponds to

**Level 2 — Anumāna / Level 3 — Logic**

## You should understand

- Anumāna
- Pakṣa
- Sādhya
- Hetu
- Vyāpti
- Sapakṣa
- Vipakṣa
- Anumiti

## You should be able to

- Construct a valid inference.
- Identify each component.
- Explain the role of *vyāpti*.
- Distinguish the inferential reason from the conclusion.
- Test an inference against possible counterexamples.

## Demonstration

Construct an inference in the following form:

```text
Pakṣa:
__________

Sādhya:
__________

Hetu:
__________

Vyāpti:
__________

Sapakṣa:
__________

Vipakṣa:
__________
```

Then explain why the inference succeeds or fails.

# Milestone 6 — Hetvābhāsa and Critical Reasoning

## Corresponds to

**Level 3 — Logic**

## You should understand

- What makes a reason defective.
- The major forms of *hetvābhāsa* encountered in the curriculum.
- The relationship between an apparent reason and a genuine reason.
- The importance of counterexamples.

## You should be able to

- Detect defective reasoning.
- Explain precisely why the reason fails.
- Distinguish an invalid argument from an argument whose conclusion happens to be true.
- Reconstruct the intended *vyāpti* and test it.

## Demonstration

Analyze five arguments and record:

```text
Claim
Reason
Intended Vyāpti
Counterexample
Defect
Final assessment
```

# Milestone 7 — Nyāya Argument Structure

## Corresponds to

**Level 3 — Logic / Level 4 — Debate**

## You should understand

- Pratijñā
- Hetu
- Udāharaṇa
- Upanaya
- Nigamana

## You should be able to

- Construct the classical five-member argument.
- Reduce it to a simpler premise-conclusion structure.
- Explain the function of each member.
- Identify what is contributed by the example and application.

## Demonstration

Take one philosophical argument and present it in both forms:

### Five-member Nyāya form

```text
Pratijñā
Hetu
Udāharaṇa
Upanaya
Nigamana
```

### Analytical form

```text
Premises
↓
Inferential relation
↓
Conclusion
```

# Milestone 8 — Philosophical Debate

## Corresponds to

**Level 4 — Debate**

## You should understand

- Vāda
- Jalpa
- Vitaṇḍā
- Tarka
- Nirṇaya
- Nigrahasthāna

## You should be able to

- Distinguish legitimate philosophical inquiry from adversarial debate.
- Identify the thesis and counter-thesis.
- Reconstruct objections and replies.
- Identify procedural and logical defects.
- Explain how a debate reaches a determination.

## Demonstration

Reconstruct a philosophical dispute as:

```text
Position A
↓
Argument
↓
Objection B
↓
Reply A
↓
Counter-objection B
↓
Further reply
↓
Assessment
```

# Milestone 9 — Upamāna

## Corresponds to

**Level 2 — Upamāna**

## You should understand

- The Nyāya account of comparison.
- The role of similarity.
- The relation between prior knowledge and the resulting cognition.
- Why *upamāna* is treated as distinct from neighboring epistemic processes.

## You should be able to

- Explain a standard example of *upamāna*.
- Identify the cognition produced.
- Explain why the cognition is not simply inference.
- Compare *upamāna* with *anumāna* and *śabda*.

# Milestone 12 — Philosophy of Language

## Corresponds to

**Level 5 — Language, Meaning and Cognition**

## You should understand

- Word
- Sentence
- Meaning
- Śakti
- Sphoṭa
- Sentence cognition
- Linguistic understanding

## You should be able to

- Explain the Nyāya approach to word and meaning.
- Explain the relevant Mīmāṃsā approach.
- Explain Bhartṛhari's position on language.
- Identify where the theories genuinely disagree.
- Avoid reducing all three positions to a modern theory of semantics.

## Demonstration

Compare:

```text
Nyāya
   ↓
Word / reference / convention

Mīmāṃsā
   ↓
Sentence / authority / interpretation

Bhartṛhari
   ↓
Śabda / Sphoṭa / sentence cognition
```

# Milestone 13 — Bhartṛhari and Vākyapadīya

## Corresponds to

**Level 5 — Vākyapadīya**

## You should understand

- The central concerns of the *Vākyapadīya*.
- Sphoṭa.
- Sentence and word.
- Language and cognition.
- The philosophical significance of linguistic unity.

## You should be able to

- Explain Bhartṛhari's position without translating it into a Nyāya framework.
- Identify the philosophical problem being addressed.
- Compare Bhartṛhari with Nyāya and Mīmāṃsā only after presenting his own position accurately.

# Milestone 14 — Ontology

## Corresponds to

**Level 6 — Ontology and Metaphysics**

## You should understand

- The Vaiśeṣika categories.
- Substance and qualities.
- Universals and particulars.
- Inherence.
- Absence.
- Causation.
- Time and space.
- Self and mind.

## You should be able to

- Explain the structure of Vaiśeṣika ontology.
- Distinguish ontological categories.
- Explain the role of relations.
- Identify the epistemological basis of an ontological claim.

## Demonstration

For every major ontological claim, ask:

```text
What is being asserted?
        ↓
What category does it belong to?
        ↓
What distinguishes it from related categories?
        ↓
How is it known?
        ↓
What objection can be raised?
```

# Milestone 15 — Self and Mind

## Corresponds to

**Level 6 — Self, Mind and Cognition**

## You should understand

- Ātman
- Manas
- Cognition
- Desire
- Pleasure
- Pain
- Memory
- Agency
- Rebirth
- Liberation

## You should be able to

- Explain the Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika distinction between self and mind.
- Explain why cognition is relevant to the analysis of the self.
- Reconstruct arguments for the existence of the self.
- Compare the classical account carefully with modern philosophy of mind.

## Important caution

Do not automatically translate:

```text
Ātman = consciousness
Manas = brain
```

Such equivalences must not be assumed.

# Milestone 17 — Navya-Nyāya

## Corresponds to

**Level 8 — Advanced Analysis**

## You should understand

- The need for technical precision.
- Qualifiers and qualified entities.
- Relations and relata.
- Limiting conditions.
- Technical analysis of inference.
- Technical analysis of perception.
- Technical analysis of absence and cognition.

## You should be able to

- Read a technical Navya-Nyāya passage slowly.
- Identify its grammatical and philosophical structure.
- Distinguish the entities and relations being analyzed.
- Reconstruct the argument without flattening its technical distinctions.

## Demonstration

Take a difficult passage and produce:

```text
Technical terminology
↓
Literal structure
↓
Philosophical structure
↓
Argument
↓
Objection
↓
Reply
```

# Milestone 18 — Comparative Philosophy

## Corresponds to

**Level 9 — Comparative Philosophy**

## You should be able to compare:

### Nyāya ↔ Vaiśeṣika

- Ontology
- Epistemology
- Causation
- Self

### Nyāya ↔ Mīmāṃsā

- Pramāṇa
- Śabda
- Prāmāṇya
- Meaning
- Authority

### Nyāya ↔ Bhartṛhari

- Language
- Meaning
- Word
- Sentence
- Cognition

## Standard of comparison

Never write:

> "Both schools basically say the same thing."

Instead write:

```text
Shared problem
↓
Position A
↓
Position B
↓
Shared assumptions
↓
Different assumptions
↓
Different reasoning
↓
Different conclusions
```

# Milestone 19 — Modern Application

## Corresponds to

**Level 9 — Comparative Philosophy**

## You should be able to apply classical analytical methods to:

- Scientific reasoning
- Legal reasoning
- Evidence evaluation
- Critical thinking
- AI
- Cognitive science
- Philosophy of mind
- Software engineering
- Decision-making

## Required discipline

Always distinguish:

```text
Classical doctrine
        ≠
Modern analogy
```

A modern example is useful only if it clarifies the structure of the classical idea without distorting it.

# Milestone 20 — Independent Philosophical Inquiry

## Corresponds to

**Level 10 — Independent Study**

At this stage you should be capable of selecting a philosophical problem and investigating it independently.

Possible questions:

- What is knowledge?
- What makes inference valid?
- What is the self?
- What is language?
- What is meaning?
- Is testimony an independent source of knowledge?
- What is a universal?
- What is causation?
- How is error possible?
- What constitutes a valid argument?

## Required research structure

```text
Question
↓
Relevant texts
↓
Terminology
↓
Position
↓
Argument
↓
Objection
↓
Reply
↓
Rival position
↓
Comparison
↓
Evaluation
↓
Conclusion
```

# Final Milestone — Philosophical Competence

The curriculum is considered substantially completed when the learner can move independently between:

```text
TEXT
  ↕
CONCEPT
  ↕
ARGUMENT
  ↕
OBJECTION
  ↕
REPLY
  ↕
COMPARISON
  ↕
CRITIQUE
  ↕
APPLICATION
```

The final standard is not:

> "I have read all the books."

The standard is:

> **"I can understand what a philosophical position claims, explain why it claims it, reconstruct its reasoning, understand the strongest objections, compare it with rival positions, and evaluate the issue independently."**

# Review Rule

If a later milestone exposes a weakness in an earlier milestone, return to the earlier material.

The curriculum is intentionally **iterative**:

```text
Learn
 ↓
Apply
 ↓
Encounter difficulty
 ↓
Return to foundation
 ↓
Relearn at greater depth
 ↓
Continue
```

Advanced understanding should continually refine earlier understanding rather than simply replace it.

---

# Completion Principle

> **Progress is measured by increasing philosophical independence, not by the number of pages completed.**
