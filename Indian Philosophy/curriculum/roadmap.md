# Roadmap

This roadmap provides the **long-term map** of the Indian Philosophy curriculum.

It is intended to answer four questions:

1. Where does the study begin?
2. What comes next?
3. Why does each stage matter?
4. What should be achieved before moving forward?

The roadmap is deliberately broader than a reading list. The objective is to progressively develop philosophical vocabulary, textual understanding, logical reasoning, comparative analysis, and independent inquiry.

# 2. Phase I — Build the Foundation

## Goal

Become comfortable with the basic vocabulary and method of philosophical inquiry.

### Main resources

```text
Tarkasaṅgraha
Nyāya Sūtras
Nyāyamañjarī
```

### Core concepts

- Darśana
- Jñāna
- Pramā
- Pramāṇa
- Prameya
- Saṃśaya
- Smṛti
- Anubhava
- Error
- Argument
- Definition

### Expected capability

You should be able to read a basic philosophical passage and identify:

```text
Problem
↓
Concepts
↓
Claim
↓
Reason
↓
Conclusion
```

---

# 3. Phase II — Learn the Ontological Vocabulary

## Goal

Understand the basic Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika way of classifying reality.

### Main resources

```text
Tarkasaṅgraha
Vaiśeṣika Sūtras
```

### Core concepts

```text
Padārtha
├── Dravya
├── Guṇa
├── Karma
├── Sāmānya
├── Viśeṣa
├── Samavāya
└── Abhāva
```

### Expected capability

You should be able to explain:

- what each category means;
- why it is required;
- what distinguishes it from other categories;
- what philosophical problems arise from the classification.

# 5. Phase IV — Master Inference

## Goal

Develop rigorous reasoning skills.

### Main resources

```text
Nyāya Sūtras
Tarkasaṅgraha
Tattvacintāmaṇi — Anumāna-khaṇḍa
```

### Study sequence

```text
Anumāna
↓
Pakṣa
↓
Sādhya
↓
Hetu
↓
Vyāpti
↓
Sapakṣa / Vipakṣa
↓
Hetvābhāsa
```

### Practical objective

Become capable of taking any ordinary argument and asking:

```text
What is being established?
What is the reason?
Why should the reason establish it?
Is the relation universal?
Are there counterexamples?
Is the reason defective?
```

# 7. Phase VI — Study Language as Knowledge

## Goal

Understand why language is both a linguistic and epistemological problem.

### Main resources

```text
Nyāya Sūtras
Tattvacintāmaṇi — Śabda-khaṇḍa
Pūrva Mīmāṃsā Sūtra
Vākyapadīya
```

### Core questions

- How does a word convey meaning?
- How does a sentence produce cognition?
- What makes testimony trustworthy?
- What is the relation between word and object?
- What is the relation between language and cognition?
- Is verbal knowledge reducible to inference?

---

# 8. Phase VII — Study Mīmāṃsā Independently

## Goal

Understand Mīmāṃsā on its own terms before comparing it with Nyāya.

### Main resource

```text
Pūrva Mīmāṃsā Sūtra
```

### Core areas

- Dharma
- Vedic authority
- Interpretation
- Sentence meaning
- Verbal authority
- Action
- Apūrva

### Required discipline

Do not approach Mīmāṃsā merely as an opponent of Nyāya.

First establish:

```text
Mīmāṃsā problem
↓
Mīmāṃsā concepts
↓
Mīmāṃsā argument
↓
Mīmāṃsā conclusion
```

Only then compare it with Nyāya.

# 10. Phase IX — Return to Ontology

## Goal

Use the epistemological and logical tools already developed to investigate metaphysical claims.

### Main resources

```text
Vaiśeṣika Sūtras
Nyāya Sūtras
Nyāyamañjarī
```

### Core areas

- Substance
- Qualities
- Action
- Universals
- Particulars
- Inherence
- Absence
- Self
- Mind
- Time
- Space
- Causation
- Dharma
- Adharma
- Liberation

### Central question

Do not merely ask:

> What exists?

Also ask:

> How is the claim that it exists justified?

# 11. Phase X — Self, Mind and Cognition

## Goal

Bring epistemology and ontology together around the knowing subject.

### Main resources

```text
Nyāya Sūtras
Vaiśeṣika Sūtras
Nyāyamañjarī
```

### Core questions

- What is the self?
- What is mind?
- What is cognition?
- What is memory?
- What is agency?
- How is continuity of the subject explained?
- What is the relation between self and body?

### Modern comparison

Only after understanding the classical position, compare with:

- Philosophy of mind
- Cognitive science
- Psychology
- Artificial intelligence

# 12. Phase XI — Enter Navya-Nyāya

## Goal

Develop technical analytical precision.

### Main resource

```text
Tattvacintāmaṇi
```

### Core skills

- Reading technical definitions
- Identifying qualifiers
- Identifying relations
- Identifying limiting conditions
- Analyzing relational cognition
- Analyzing absence
- Analyzing inferential structure
- Tracking technical distinctions

### Study method

```text
Technical passage
↓
Terminology
↓
Literal structure
↓
Philosophical structure
↓
Argument
↓
Objection
↓
Reply
```

This phase should be slow.

Do not attempt to read Navya-Nyāya technical material at the same speed as introductory material.

---

# 13. Phase XII — Comparative Philosophy

## Goal

Learn to compare traditions without collapsing their differences.

### Major comparisons

```text
Nyāya
   ↕
Vaiśeṣika

Nyāya
   ↕
Mīmāṃsā

Nyāya
   ↕
Bhartṛhari

Mīmāṃsā
   ↕
Bhartṛhari
```

### Compare using a fixed framework

For every comparison ask:

1. What problem is being addressed?
2. What does each tradition claim?
3. What assumptions does each make?
4. What argument supports each position?
5. What objections are raised?
6. How does each tradition respond?
7. Where do they genuinely agree?
8. Where do they fundamentally disagree?

# 14. Phase XIII — Modern Applications

## Goal

Bring classical analytical methods into contemporary intellectual contexts.

### Areas

- Artificial intelligence
- Cognitive science
- Philosophy of mind
- Software engineering
- Mathematics
- Law
- Decision theory
- Critical thinking
- Scientific reasoning

### Application framework

```text
Classical concept
↓
Classical argument
↓
Structural analysis
↓
Modern problem
↓
Possible application
↓
Limitations
```

### Important rule

Do not claim:

> "Ancient theory X is exactly the same as modern theory Y."

Instead ask:

> "Is there a useful structural analogy between X and Y, and where does the analogy fail?"

# 15. Phase XIV — Independent Inquiry

## Goal

Become capable of conducting philosophical research independently.

Choose a question and investigate it across the repository.

### Example questions

- What is knowledge?
- What makes inference valid?
- What is the self?
- What is language?
- What is meaning?
- What makes testimony authoritative?
- What is a universal?
- What is causation?
- How is error possible?
- What makes an argument fallacious?

### Research sequence

```text
QUESTION
   ↓
LOCATE RELEVANT BOOKS
   ↓
IDENTIFY TECHNICAL TERMS
   ↓
READ RELEVANT PASSAGES
   ↓
RECONSTRUCT POSITION
   ↓
RECONSTRUCT ARGUMENT
   ↓
IDENTIFY OBJECTIONS
   ↓
IDENTIFY REPLIES
   ↓
COMPARE RIVAL POSITIONS
   ↓
EVALUATE
   ↓
FORMULATE CONCLUSION
```

# 18. Depth Levels

Each major topic should ideally be studied at three depths.

## Level A — Recognition

You can identify the term and give its basic meaning.

Example:

> *Vyāpti* = invariable concomitance.

## Level B — Understanding

You can explain:

- what the concept does;
- why it is needed;
- how it relates to other concepts.

## Level C — Analysis

You can:

- reconstruct the philosophical argument;
- understand objections;
- compare rival accounts;
- identify assumptions;
- evaluate the reasoning.

The goal of the curriculum is to move every major concept from **A → B → C**.

---

# 19. Revision and Recursion

The roadmap is not a one-way path.

Later study should change the understanding of earlier material.

```text
First reading
    ↓
Basic understanding
    ↓
Advanced study
    ↓
New distinctions
    ↓
Return to earlier text
    ↓
Deeper interpretation
```

For example:

```text
Tarkasaṅgraha
    ↓
Tattvacintāmaṇi
    ↓
Return to Tarkasaṅgraha
    ↓
Notice distinctions previously overlooked
```

This recursive process is expected.

---

# 20. Final Destination

The endpoint of the roadmap is not the completion of the final book.

The endpoint is the ability to independently move between:

```text
TEXT
 ↕
TERMINOLOGY
 ↕
CONCEPT
 ↕
ARGUMENT
 ↕
OBJECTION
 ↕
REPLY
 ↕
COMPARISON
 ↕
CRITIQUE
 ↕
APPLICATION
 ↕
INDEPENDENT INQUIRY
```

The ultimate objective is to develop a disciplined philosophical habit:

> **Read precisely. Understand before judging. Reconstruct before criticizing. Compare without conflating. Question assumptions. Follow arguments to their consequences.**

---

# 21. Roadmap Principle

> **The curriculum is a progression from learning the vocabulary of philosophical inquiry to becoming capable of conducting philosophical inquiry independently.**
