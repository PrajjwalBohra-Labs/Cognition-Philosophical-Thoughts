# Learning Path

This document defines the **practical sequence of study** for the Indian Philosophy curriculum.

It explains not only *what* to study, but **why each stage comes before the next**, which books should be used, what capabilities should be developed, and when to move from one stage to another.

The learning path is designed around the books in `books/` and the broader curriculum in `curriculum.md`.

---

# 1. Overall Progression

The complete path follows this progression:

```text
FOUNDATIONS
    │
    ▼
CATEGORIES AND ONTOLOGY
    │
    ▼
EPISTEMOLOGY
    │
    ▼
INFERENCE AND LOGIC
    │
    ▼
DEBATE AND ARGUMENTATION
    │
    ▼
LANGUAGE AND TESTIMONY
    │
    ▼
MĪMĀṂSĀ
    │
    ▼
COGNITION, SELF AND METAPHYSICS
    │
    ▼
NAVYA-NYĀYA
    │
    ▼
COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS
    │
    ▼
INDEPENDENT PHILOSOPHICAL INQUIRY
```

The progression is deliberate.

A student should first acquire the vocabulary and conceptual distinctions required to understand later arguments. Technical analysis should come only after the underlying philosophical problems are clear.

---

# 2. Stage I — Orientation and Foundations

## Purpose

Build the basic conceptual vocabulary required for reading Indian philosophical texts.

### Study

- What is *darśana*?
- What is philosophical inquiry?
- What is knowledge?
- What is cognition?
- What is doubt?
- What is error?
- What is a valid means of knowledge?
- What is an object of knowledge?

### Primary resources

```text
books/nyaya/tarkasangraha/
books/nyaya/nyaya-sutras/
books/nyaya/nyayamanjari/
```

### Main outcome

You should be able to distinguish:

```text
Question
   ↓
Claim
   ↓
Reason
   ↓
Argument
   ↓
Conclusion
```

and understand why a philosophical conclusion requires justification.

---

# 3. Stage II — Categories and Ontological Vocabulary

## Purpose

Acquire the conceptual framework needed to understand how Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika describes reality.

### Study

- Padārtha
- Dravya
- Guṇa
- Karma
- Sāmānya
- Viśeṣa
- Samavāya
- Abhāva

### Primary resources

```text
books/nyaya/tarkasangraha/
books/vaisesika/vaisesika-sutra/
```

### Study method

Do not merely memorize the categories.

For every category ask:

1. What is it?
2. Why is it needed?
3. What examples belong to it?
4. What examples do not belong to it?
5. How is it distinguished from related categories?
6. What philosophical problem does it solve?

### Main outcome

You should be able to explain the Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika category system without relying on a memorized list.

---

# 4. Stage III — Epistemology

## Purpose

Understand how valid cognition is produced and how knowledge is distinguished from doubt, memory, and error.

### Study order

```text
Pramā
   ↓
Pramāṇa
   ↓
Prameya
   ↓
Prāmāṇya
   ↓
Four Nyāya Pramāṇas
```

Then study:

```text
Pratyakṣa
   ↓
Anumāna
   ↓
Upamāna
   ↓
Śabda
```

### Primary resources

```text
books/nyaya/nyaya-sutras/
books/nyaya/tarkasangraha/
books/nyaya/tattvacintamani/
```

### Main outcome

You should be able to explain why Nyāya recognizes particular means of knowledge and what conditions make cognition valid.

---

# 5. Stage IV — Pratyakṣa

## Purpose

Develop a precise understanding of perception before moving deeply into inference.

### Study

- Perception
- Sense-object relation
- Cognition
- Conditions of perceptual knowledge
- Valid perception
- Error
- Ordinary and extraordinary issues in perception

### Primary resources

```text
books/nyaya/nyaya-sutras/
books/nyaya/tattvacintamani/
```

Give particular attention to:

```text
Tattvacintāmaṇi
└── Pratyakṣa-khaṇḍa
```

### Main outcome

You should be able to analyze a perceptual cognition in terms of its object, conditions, and epistemic status.

---

# 6. Stage V — Anumāna and Logic

## Purpose

This is the central transition from basic epistemology to rigorous analytical reasoning.

### Study order

```text
Anumāna
   ↓
Pakṣa
   ↓
Sādhya
   ↓
Hetu
   ↓
Vyāpti
   ↓
Sapakṣa
   ↓
Vipakṣa
   ↓
Hetvābhāsa
```

### Primary resources

```text
books/nyaya/nyaya-sutras/
books/nyaya/tarkasangraha/
books/nyaya/tattvacintamani/
```

Give particular attention to:

```text
Tattvacintāmaṇi
└── Anumāna-khaṇḍa
```

### Practice

Construct arguments from ordinary situations.

For example:

```text
Pakṣa:
The hill

Sādhya:
Possession of fire

Hetu:
Possession of smoke

Vyāpti:
Wherever there is smoke, there is fire
```

Then ask whether the *vyāpti* is actually established.

### Main outcome

You should be able to:

- reconstruct an inference;
- identify its components;
- examine its inferential relation;
- identify possible counterexamples;
- diagnose defective reasoning.

---

# 7. Stage VI — Upamāna

## Purpose

Understand comparison as a distinct source of knowledge and distinguish it from inference and testimony.

### Primary resource

```text
books/nyaya/tattvacintamani/
└── Upamāna-khaṇḍa
```

### Questions

- What exactly produces the cognition?
- Why is the cognition not simply inference?
- What role does similarity play?
- What role does prior verbal knowledge play?

### Main outcome

You should be able to explain the Nyāya account of *upamāna* and distinguish it from neighboring epistemic processes.

---

# 8. Stage VII — Debate and Argumentation

## Purpose

Move from analyzing individual arguments to analyzing philosophical disagreement as a structured activity.

### Study

- Vāda
- Jalpa
- Vitaṇḍā
- Tarka
- Nirṇaya
- Hetvābhāsa
- Nigrahasthāna
- Objection
- Reply
- Counter-reply

### Primary resources

```text
books/nyaya/nyaya-sutras/
books/nyaya/nyayamanjari/
```

### Practice

Take a philosophical dispute and reconstruct it as:

```text
Position A
    ↓
Argument
    ↓
Objection B
    ↓
Reply A
    ↓
Counter-objection B
    ↓
Further reply
```

### Main outcome

You should be able to distinguish genuine philosophical disagreement from merely rhetorical disagreement.

---

# 9. Stage VIII — Śabda and Philosophy of Language

## Purpose

Understand verbal knowledge and prepare for the deeper linguistic analysis of Mīmāṃsā and Bhartṛhari.

### Study

- Śabda
- Śabdapramāṇa
- Āpta
- Word
- Meaning
- Sentence
- Convention
- Speaker
- Verbal cognition

### Primary resources

```text
books/nyaya/tattvacintamani/
books/nyaya/nyaya-sutras/
books/mimamsa/purva-mimamsa-sutra/
books/vyakarana/vakyapadiya/
```

### Recommended sequence

```text
Nyāya account
    ↓
Mīmāṃsā account
    ↓
Bhartṛhari
    ↓
Comparative analysis
```

### Main outcome

You should be able to explain why language becomes an epistemological problem and why different traditions develop different theories of verbal cognition and meaning.

---

# 10. Stage IX — Mīmāṃsā

## Purpose

Study Mīmāṃsā as an independent philosophical system before using it merely as a source of objections to Nyāya.

### Study

- Dharma
- Vedic authority
- Injunction
- Interpretation
- Verbal authority
- Sentence meaning
- Action
- Apūrva
- Epistemological questions

### Primary resource

```text
books/mimamsa/purva-mimamsa-sutra/
```

### Comparative reading

After studying the Mīmāṃsā position independently, compare it with Nyāya.

```text
Mīmāṃsā
    ↕
Nyāya
```

Focus especially on:

- Śabda
- Prāmāṇya
- Meaning
- Authority
- Veda
- Sentence cognition

### Main outcome

You should be able to present a Mīmāṃsā position accurately even when you disagree with it.

---

# 11. Stage X — Vākyapadīya and Bhartṛhari

## Purpose

Move into the philosophical analysis of language, meaning, cognition, and linguistic unity.

### Study order

```text
Vākyapadīya
    │
    ├── Kāṇḍa I
    │
    ├── Kāṇḍa II
    │
    └── Kāṇḍa III
```

### Primary resources

```text
books/vyakarana/vakyapadiya/
```

### Study

- Śabda
- Sphoṭa
- Word
- Sentence
- Meaning
- Sentence cognition
- Language and thought
- Linguistic unity
- Relation between language and reality

### Main outcome

You should be able to explain Bhartṛhari's position on language on its own terms before comparing it with Nyāya or Mīmāṃsā.

---

# 12. Stage XI — Ontology and Metaphysics

## Purpose

Return to the questions of reality after acquiring sufficient epistemological and logical tools.

### Study

- Self
- Mind
- Substance
- Qualities
- Universals
- Particulars
- Inherence
- Absence
- Causation
- Time
- Space
- Action
- Dharma
- Adharma
- Liberation

### Primary resources

```text
books/vaisesika/vaisesika-sutra/
books/nyaya/nyaya-sutras/
books/nyaya/nyayamanjari/
```

### Why this stage comes later

Metaphysical claims should now be examined together with the epistemological question:

> **How do we know that this metaphysical claim is justified?**

This prevents ontology from being studied as an uncritical list of entities.

---

# 13. Stage XII — Self, Mind and Cognition

## Purpose

Bring epistemology and ontology together in the analysis of the knowing subject.

### Study

- Ātman
- Manas
- Cognition
- Memory
- Desire
- Pleasure
- Pain
- Agency
- Identity
- Rebirth
- Liberation

### Primary resources

```text
books/nyaya/nyaya-sutras/
books/vaisesika/vaisesika-sutra/
books/nyaya/nyayamanjari/
```

### Modern comparative fields

Only after understanding the classical positions, investigate comparisons with:

- Philosophy of mind
- Cognitive science
- Psychology
- Artificial intelligence

Do not assume that *ātman* simply means "consciousness" or that *manas* simply means "brain."

---

# 14. Stage XIII — Navya-Nyāya

## Purpose

Develop advanced analytical precision.

This is where the earlier stages converge.

### Primary resources

```text
books/nyaya/tattvacintamani/
```

### Study

- Technical definitions
- Relations
- Qualifiers
- Qualified entities
- Limiting conditions
- Absence
- Inferential relations
- Epistemic relations
- Precise philosophical description

### Method

For every technical passage:

```text
Read
 ↓
Identify terms
 ↓
Identify grammatical structure
 ↓
Identify philosophical claim
 ↓
Identify relation being analyzed
 ↓
Reconstruct the argument
 ↓
Compare interpretations
```

### Main outcome

You should be able to follow increasingly technical Navya-Nyāya analysis without reducing it to ordinary-language paraphrase.

---

# 15. Stage XIV — Comparative Philosophy

## Purpose

Develop the ability to compare philosophical systems accurately.

### Comparisons

#### Nyāya ↔ Vaiśeṣika

Focus on:

- Ontology
- Categories
- Epistemology
- Causation
- Self
- Liberation

#### Nyāya ↔ Mīmāṃsā

Focus on:

- Pramāṇa
- Śabda
- Prāmāṇya
- Veda
- Meaning
- Interpretation

#### Nyāya ↔ Bhartṛhari

Focus on:

- Word
- Sentence
- Meaning
- Cognition
- Language
- Reference

#### Mīmāṃsā ↔ Bhartṛhari

Focus on:

- Sentence meaning
- Linguistic cognition
- Authority
- Word
- Language

---

# 16. Stage XV — Modern Application

## Purpose

Connect classical analytical methods with contemporary intellectual problems without erasing historical differences.

### Areas

- Scientific reasoning
- Legal reasoning
- Evidence analysis
- Critical thinking
- Artificial intelligence
- Cognitive science
- Philosophy of mind
- Software engineering
- Mathematics
- Decision theory

### Method

Always use this sequence:

```text
Classical position
        ↓
Classical argument
        ↓
Structural analysis
        ↓
Modern problem
        ↓
Possible correspondence
        ↓
Limits of correspondence
```

Never begin with a modern analogy and force the classical material into it.

---

# 17. Stage XVI — Independent Study

At this stage, the learner should stop depending entirely on a predetermined sequence.

Choose a philosophical question and trace it across the collection.

Possible questions:

- What is knowledge?
- What makes inference valid?
- What is the self?
- What is the nature of language?
- How is meaning communicated?
- Is testimony an independent source of knowledge?
- What is a universal?
- What is causation?
- What makes an argument fallacious?
- How can error be explained?

### Research pattern

```text
QUESTION
   ↓
TEXTUAL EVIDENCE
   ↓
POSITION
   ↓
ARGUMENT
   ↓
OBJECTION
   ↓
REPLY
   ↓
COMPARISON
   ↓
EVALUATION
```

---

# 18. When to Move Forward

Do not advance simply because a chapter has been read.

Move to the next stage when you can perform the relevant skill independently.

## Minimum readiness criteria

### Foundations

You can explain the major technical vocabulary without memorizing isolated definitions.

### Epistemology

You can distinguish the principal pramāṇas and explain their basic operation.

### Logic

You can reconstruct an inference and identify its components.

### Debate

You can distinguish a thesis, objection, reply, and defective argument.

### Language

You can distinguish the major approaches to verbal cognition and meaning.

### Metaphysics

You can distinguish ontological categories and explain why they are posited.

### Navya-Nyāya

You can follow technical analytical distinctions without replacing them with vague paraphrases.

### Comparative study

You can state a rival school's position accurately before criticizing it.

---

# 19. Revision Cycle

The learning path is not strictly linear.

After completing an advanced stage, return to earlier works.

For example:

```text
Tattvacintāmaṇi
      ↓
Return to Nyāya Sūtras
      ↓
Re-read Anumāna
      ↓
Return to Tattvacintāmaṇi
      ↓
Reconstruct the argument at a higher level
```

This iterative movement is essential because later technical concepts often reveal distinctions that were not visible during the first reading.

---

# 20. Final Learning State

The learning path is complete when the learner can independently move between:

```text
TEXT
  ↕
CONCEPT
  ↕
ARGUMENT
  ↕
OBJECTION
  ↕
COMPARISON
  ↕
ANALYSIS
  ↕
APPLICATION
```

The final goal is not simply to know what the traditions say.

It is to be able to **read carefully, reason rigorously, distinguish philosophical positions, reconstruct arguments, identify assumptions, evaluate objections, and conduct independent philosophical inquiry**.
