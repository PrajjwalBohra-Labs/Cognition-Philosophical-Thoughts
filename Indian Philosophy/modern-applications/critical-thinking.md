# Critical Thinking

Critical thinking is the practical discipline of examining claims, evidence, assumptions, inference, alternatives, and consequences before accepting a conclusion.

The Nyāya tradition provides especially useful tools for developing this discipline.

---

# 1. Basic Workflow

Use:

```text
Claim
 ↓
Clarify terms
 ↓
Identify evidence
 ↓
Identify reasoning
 ↓
Identify assumptions
 ↓
Test inference
 ↓
Search for counterexamples
 ↓
Consider alternatives
 ↓
Conclude proportionally
```

---

# 2. Claim Types

Before evaluating a statement, classify it.

### Descriptive

> What is the case?

### Causal

> What caused it?

### Normative

> What should be done?

### Conceptual

> What does X mean?

### Predictive

> What will happen?

### Metaphysical

> What fundamentally exists?

Different claims require different forms of support.

---

# 3. Argument Reconstruction

Never evaluate a vague argument.

Rewrite it:

```text
Premise 1:
Premise 2:
Premise 3:

Therefore:
Conclusion
```

Then identify hidden premises.

---

# 4. Nyāya-Inspired Analysis

Use:

```text
Pakṣa:
What is under discussion?

Sādhya:
What is being established?

Hetu:
What reason is given?

Vyāpti:
What general relation is assumed?

Sapakṣa:
Where does the relevant relation hold?

Vipakṣa:
Where should it fail?

Counterexample:
Does the relation break?
```

This is particularly useful for causal and categorical claims.

---

# 5. Definitions

Many disagreements are actually disagreements about terminology.

For every important term ask:

- What does it mean?
- Is the meaning fixed?
- Is it being used consistently?
- Is the definition too broad?
- Is it too narrow?
- Are two people using the same word differently?

A strong definition should exclude inappropriate cases and include appropriate ones.

---

# 6. Evidence

Classify evidence by source.

```text
Direct observation
Inference
Testimony
Measurement
Document
Experiment
Calculation
Memory
Analogy
```

Then ask:

> Why should this source be trusted in this context?

---

# 7. Counterexamples

Counterexamples are among the most powerful tools in analytical thinking.

Given:

> All X are Y.

Search for:

> An X that is not Y.

But verify that the alleged counterexample genuinely belongs to X.

---

# 8. Fallacy Analysis

Do not merely name a fallacy.

Explain:

```text
Claim
↓
Reason
↓
Intended inference
↓
Failure
↓
Why the failure matters
```

A conclusion can be true even if the argument supporting it is defective.

---

# 9. Steelmanning

Before criticizing a position:

1. State it accurately.
2. Remove accidental weaknesses.
3. Present the strongest reasonable version.
4. Identify its best evidence.
5. Then criticize it.

This practice complements the classical requirement to understand the opponent's position before answering.

---

# 10. Uncertainty

Avoid false precision.

Use distinctions such as:

```text
Certain
Highly supported
Plausible
Possible
Underdetermined
Unknown
Refuted
```

The exact labels should match the evidence available.

---

# 11. Decision Under Uncertainty

Separate:

```text
What is true?
        from
What should I do?
```

A person can reasonably act under uncertainty.

This distinction becomes important in law, science, medicine, engineering, and policy.

---

# 12. Practical Daily Exercise

Take one claim from news, social media, work, or conversation.

Write:

```text
Claim:
Meaning:
Evidence:
Source:
Premises:
Hidden assumption:
Inferential relation:
Counterexample:
Alternative explanation:
Confidence:
Conclusion:
```

Do this regularly.

---

# 13. Critical-Thinking Checklist

Before accepting a claim:

- [ ] Are the terms clear?
- [ ] Is the source identified?
- [ ] Is the evidence relevant?
- [ ] Is the evidence sufficient?
- [ ] Is the reasoning valid?
- [ ] Is there a hidden assumption?
- [ ] Is there a counterexample?
- [ ] Is there an alternative explanation?
- [ ] Does the conclusion exceed the evidence?
- [ ] What would change my mind?

# Core Principle

> **Do not ask only, “Is this conclusion true?” Ask, “What are the grounds for believing it, and do those grounds actually support it?”**
