# Epistemology

Epistemology (*pramāṇa-śāstra* in a broad descriptive sense) studies knowledge, valid cognition, its sources, its objects, and the conditions under which cognition is trustworthy.

The repository's central epistemological materials come especially from Nyāya, Vaiśeṣika, Mīmāṃsā, and their debates.

---

# 1. Fundamental Questions

- What is knowledge?
- What distinguishes knowledge from error?
- What is doubt?
- What is memory?
- What is a means of knowledge?
- What is an object of knowledge?
- What makes cognition valid?
- Is validity intrinsic or established through another cognition?
- Are all sources of knowledge reducible to one fundamental source?

# 3. Nyāya's Four Pramāṇas

The classical Nyāya framework represented in the collection recognizes:

```text
Pramāṇa
│
├── Pratyakṣa — perception
├── Anumāna — inference
├── Upamāna — comparison
└── Śabda — testimony
```

Each must be studied independently before comparing them.

---

# 4. Pratyakṣa

Perception is analyzed through the relevant relation between the senses, their objects, and cognition.

The Nyāya texts emphasize that perception cannot simply be reduced to inference. Among the distinctions they draw are:

- perception's connection with the senses;
- inference's dependence upon an inferential relation;
- inference's applicability to matters not presently perceived;
- the role of recollection in inference.

---

# 5. Anumāna

Inference is cognition based on an appropriate inferential relation.

Core structure:

```text
Pakṣa
+
Hetu
+
Vyāpti
      ↓
Anumiti
```

Important questions:

- How is *vyāpti* known?
- What makes a *hetu* valid?
- How does the reason belong to the subject?
- What defeats an inference?
- Can an inference concern an imperceptible object?

The *Tattvacintāmaṇi — Anumāna-khaṇḍa* provides the technical level of analysis needed for advanced study.

---

# 6. Upamāna

*Upamāna* concerns cognition produced through comparison.

A classical problem is:

> How does knowledge of similarity contribute to recognizing the relation between a word and an object?

Nyāya treats this as distinct from ordinary perception and inference, while Vaiśeṣika commentary in the repository records a rival attempt to subsume comparison under inference.

This disagreement is useful for learning how pramāṇa classification works.

---

# 7. Śabda

*Śabda* concerns verbal cognition.

Nyāya's account emphasizes trustworthy testimony.

Important concepts:

- *Āpta* — reliable speaker
- statement
- word
- sentence
- meaning
- verbal cognition

The Nyāya material defines the trustworthy speaker in terms of knowledge and the capacity to communicate the relevant reality rather than merely absence of deception.

---

# 8. Epistemic Statuses

A cognition may need to be classified as:

```text
Cognition
│
├── Valid
├── Erroneous
├── Doubtful
└── Recollective
```

The classification matters because the same object may be associated with different cognitive states.

---

# 9. Error

Error is not simply "being wrong."

The philosophical problem is:

> How can a cognition appear as cognition of an object while failing to correspond appropriately to reality?

Study error together with:

- doubt;
- perception;
- memory;
- recognition;
- absence;
- conceptual determination.

---

# 10. Prāmāṇya

The question of validity asks what makes cognition knowledge-producing.

A useful analytical sequence is:

```text
Cognition occurs
      ↓
What produced it?
      ↓
Are the producing conditions appropriate?
      ↓
Does it correspond to its object?
      ↓
Can its validity be established?
```

Different traditions disagree about the precise account of epistemic validity and authority.

Do not collapse these disagreements.

# 12. Epistemology and Ontology

Epistemology cannot be isolated from ontology.

For example:

> If the self is not perceptible, how can its existence be established?

The Nyāya texts explore inferential routes to supersensible objects, while also analyzing what counts as a legitimate subject of inference.

This creates a general methodological loop:

```text
Theory of knowledge
       ↓
Claims about reality
       ↓
Arguments for those claims
       ↓
Evaluation of the means of knowledge
       ↓
Refined theory of knowledge
```

---

# 13. Epistemology and Cognition

The study of cognition asks what occurs.

Epistemology asks when the occurrence qualifies as knowledge.

Thus:

```text
Cognition
   ↓
Epistemic evaluation
   ↓
Valid / invalid / doubtful / recollective
```

---

# 14. Modern Application

Useful comparison areas include:

- evidence evaluation;
- scientific inference;
- testimony and expertise;
- legal evidence;
- eyewitness testimony;
- information verification;
- machine-generated information.

Do not equate classical *pramāṇa* theory with modern epistemology. Use modern cases as analytical exercises.

## Core Principle

> **The central epistemological question is not merely whether someone believes something, but what makes the cognition knowledge.**
