# Books

This directory is the **core library** of the Indian Philosophy repository.

It contains the books, editions, translations, commentaries, and scholarly works used as the primary textual resources for studying Indian philosophy.

The collection is organized primarily by **philosophical tradition**, rather than by chronology or subject alone. This makes it possible to study individual traditions internally while also tracing philosophical problems across traditions.

---

## Purpose

The `books/` directory is intended to:

- Maintain a structured personal library of Indian philosophical literature.
- Keep works belonging to related traditions together.
- Provide the textual foundation for the curriculum.
- Support comparative study across philosophical schools.
- Preserve bibliographic and edition information.
- Make the collection easy to expand as new works are acquired.

This directory is the **source library** of the repository. Interpretations, applications, diagrams, and supplementary resources should be maintained in their respective directories rather than mixed into the core book collection.

## 1.1 Nyāya Sūtras

```text
nyaya/nyaya-sutras/
```

This folder contains the collected volumes of the **Nyāya Sūtras of Gautama** and their associated explanatory material.

The collection includes the four-volume set:

- *Nyāya Sūtras of Gautama — Volume I*
- *Nyāya Sūtras of Gautama — Volume II*
- *Nyāya Sūtras of Gautama — Volume III*
- *Nyāya Sūtras of Gautama — Volume IV*

The editions in the collection include material associated with:

- Gautama
- Vātsyāyana
- Uddyotakara
- Vācaspati Miśra
- Udayana
- Other associated commentators and explanatory traditions

These volumes form one of the principal foundations for studying classical Nyāya.

---

## 1.2 Tattvacintāmaṇi

```text
nyaya/tattvacintamani/
```

This folder contains the available sections and editions of **Gaṅgeśa Upādhyāya's Tattvacintāmaṇi** and associated commentarial material.

The collection covers the principal pramāṇa investigations represented in the uploaded works:

### Pratyakṣa-khaṇḍa

Study of perception and the conditions, structure, and validity of perceptual cognition.

### Anumāna-khaṇḍa

Study of inference, including:

- Hetu
- Vyāpti
- Pakṣa
- Sādhya
- Inference structures
- Relations relevant to inferential cognition

### Upamāna-khaṇḍa

Study of comparison and the cognition generated through the relevant relation between an already known object and something to be learned.

### Śabda-khaṇḍa

Study of verbal testimony and linguistic knowledge, including questions concerning the validity and operation of verbal cognition.

The *Tattvacintāmaṇi* material represents an important stage in the development of **Navya-Nyāya analytical terminology and method**.

---

## 1.3 Tarkasaṅgraha

```text
nyaya/tarkasangraha/
```

Contains **Tarkasaṅgraha of Annambhaṭṭa**, an important systematic work on Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika categories and terminology.

It is particularly useful as an entry point into:

- Padārthas
- Dravya
- Guṇa
- Karma
- Sāmānya
- Viśeṣa
- Samavāya
- Abhāva
- Basic epistemological and logical terminology

It should therefore be treated as one of the foundational works of the collection.

---

## 1.4 Nyāyamañjarī

```text
nyaya/nyayamanjari/
```

Contains **Nyāyamañjarī of Jayanta Bhaṭṭa**, an extensive work of Nyāya philosophical analysis and debate.

This work is important for studying Nyāya as a broad philosophical system rather than merely as a technical theory of inference.

It provides material relevant to:

- Epistemology
- Logic
- Debate
- Philosophical objections
- Responses to rival positions
- Metaphysics
- Knowledge
- Self
- Causation
- The broader Nyāya philosophical worldview

# Cross-Traditional Reading

The organization of this directory should not imply that each tradition is philosophically isolated.

Many of the most important questions in Indian philosophy are investigated by multiple traditions from different perspectives.

For example:

```text
                    KNOWLEDGE
                       │
        ┌──────────────┼──────────────┐
        │              │              │
      Nyāya         Mīmāṃsā      Vyākaraṇa
        │              │              │
    Pramāṇa         Śabda        Language
        │              │              │
    Anumāna        Authority      Meaning
    Pratyakṣa      Interpretation Cognition
    Upamāna
    Śabda
        │
        └──────────────┐
                       │
                  Vaiśeṣika
                       │
                   Ontology
                       │
                  Padārthas
```

When studying a philosophical problem, relevant works from different folders should therefore be consulted together.

# Suggested Reading Relationship

The books are not arranged strictly as a chronological history.

Instead, they can be approached according to increasing philosophical and analytical complexity.

A useful relationship between the current collection is:

```text
Tarkasaṅgraha
      │
      ▼
Nyāya Sūtras
      │
      ├──────────────► Vaiśeṣika Sūtras
      │
      ▼
Nyāyamañjarī
      │
      ▼
Tattvacintāmaṇi
      │
      ├── Pratyakṣa
      ├── Anumāna
      ├── Upamāna
      └── Śabda
             │
             ├──────────────► Mīmāṃsā
             │
             └──────────────► Vākyapadīya
```

This is a **study relationship**, not a claim that the texts form a single linear philosophical development.

# How to Add Books

When adding a new book, first determine its primary philosophical affiliation.

For example:

```text
Nyāya       → books/nyaya/
Vaiśeṣika   → books/vaisesika/
Mīmāṃsā     → books/mimamsa/
Vyākaraṇa   → books/vyakarana/
```

If the work belongs to a specific existing category, place it in the most appropriate subdirectory.

If a new tradition becomes sufficiently represented in the collection, create a new top-level directory rather than forcing the work into an unsuitable category.

---

# File Naming Convention

Use descriptive filenames that preserve bibliographic information.

Preferred:

```text
Author_Title_Volume.pdf
```

or:

```text
Title_Khanda_Edition.pdf
```

Examples:

```text
Nyaya_Sutras_Gautama_Vol_1.pdf
Tattvacintamani_Pratyaksha_Khanda.pdf
Tattvacintamani_Anumana_Khanda.pdf
Tarkasangraha_Annambhatta.pdf
Nyayamanjari_Jayanta_Bhatta.pdf
Vaisesika_Sutras_Kanada.pdf
Purva_Mimamsa_Sutra_Jaimini.pdf
Vakyapadiya_Kanda_1_Bhartrhari.pdf
```

Avoid ambiguous names such as:

```text
book.pdf
newbook.pdf
final.pdf
book_final_new2.pdf
scan.pdf
```

Where the original edition has important information about the translator, editor, commentator, publication year, or volume, preserve that information in the filename or in the bibliography.

---

# Edition and Source Information

Different editions of the same work may contain substantially different:

- Translations
- Commentaries
- Introductions
- Annotations
- Textual readings
- Pagination
- Editorial decisions

Therefore, do not replace an existing edition merely because another edition becomes available.

When multiple editions are kept, identify them clearly.

For example:

```text
Tattvacintamani_Pratyaksha_Khanda_Edition_A.pdf
Tattvacintamani_Pratyaksha_Khanda_Edition_B.pdf
```

The corresponding bibliographic information should be recorded in:

```text
reference/bibliography.md
```

---

# Principle of Organization

The organization of the library follows three principles:

### 1. Tradition

Books are grouped according to their principal philosophical tradition.

### 2. Intellectual Relationship

Closely related works are grouped together so that they can be studied comparatively.

### 3. Bibliographic Integrity

The identity of each edition should be preserved rather than reducing all versions of a work to a single generic title.

---

# Long-Term Expansion

The current structure is intentionally expandable.

As the collection grows, additional traditions and works can be incorporated without disturbing the existing organization.

Possible future additions may include dedicated sections for other major Indian philosophical traditions or additional works within the existing traditions.

The structure should therefore evolve according to the actual collection rather than creating empty categories prematurely.

---

## Core Principle

> **The `books/` directory is the textual foundation of the repository.**

The goal is not simply to accumulate books, but to maintain an organized philosophical library in which texts can be located, compared, studied, and connected across traditions.
