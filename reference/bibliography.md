# Bibliography

This directory is the reference layer of the repository. It records the books and editions available in the collection, their principal philosophical roles, and the way they should be used in study.

The bibliography is **repository-specific**: it describes the texts actually available in this project rather than attempting to provide an exhaustive bibliography of Indian philosophy.

---

# 1. Primary Repository Collection

## 1.1 Nyāya

### *Nyāya Sūtras of Gautama* — Volumes I–IV

**Tradition:** Nyāya  
**Author:** Gautama / Akṣapāda  
**Primary use:** Logic, epistemology, inference, debate, argumentation, metaphysics, self, liberation.

Use these volumes for the foundational structure of Nyāya before moving to later technical analysis.

---

### *Nyāyamañjarī* — Jayanta Bhaṭṭa

**Author:** Jayanta Bhaṭṭa  
**Tradition:** Nyāya  
**Primary use:** Broad philosophical analysis, objections and replies, epistemology, metaphysics, debate, rival schools.

This is particularly valuable after acquiring basic Nyāya terminology because it presents philosophical issues in a broader argumentative environment.

---

### *Tarkasaṅgraha of Annambhaṭṭa*

**Author:** Annambhaṭṭa  
**Tradition:** Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika  
**Primary use:** Foundational categories, epistemology, ontology, terminology, introductory systematic structure.

Use it as a compact conceptual map before entering more technical works.

---

# 2. Tattvacintāmaṇi Collection

## *Tattvacintāmaṇi — Pratyakṣa-khaṇḍa*

**Author:** Gaṅgeśa Upādhyāya  
**Tradition:** Navya-Nyāya  
**Primary use:** Advanced analysis of perception and perceptual cognition.

---

## *Tattvacintāmaṇi — Anumāna-khaṇḍa*

**Author:** Gaṅgeśa Upādhyāya  
**Tradition:** Navya-Nyāya  
**Primary use:** Advanced inference, *hetu*, *vyāpti*, inferential cognition, defects of inference.

This is one of the central advanced reasoning texts in the repository.

---

## *Tattvacintāmaṇi — Upamāna-khaṇḍa*

**Author:** Gaṅgeśa Upādhyāya  
**Tradition:** Navya-Nyāya  
**Primary use:** Comparison and the epistemological status of *upamāna*.

Use it comparatively with Nyāya and Vaiśeṣika treatments of pramāṇa.

---

## *Tattvacintāmaṇi — Śabda / Āloka material*

**Primary use:** Verbal cognition, testimony, language, authority, and technical analysis of *śabda*.

The repository also contains the *Āloka* material associated with the analysis of the relevant Nyāya discussion.

---

# 3. Vaiśeṣika

## *Vaiśeṣika Sūtras of Kaṇāda*

**Author:** Kaṇāda  
**Tradition:** Vaiśeṣika  
**Primary use:** Ontology, categories, substances, qualities, action, universals, particularity, inherence, absence, causation, self, mind, time and space.

This is the principal source for studying the ontological architecture underlying later Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika analysis.

---

# 4. Mīmāṃsā

## *Pūrva Mīmāṃsā Sūtra of Jaimini with the Original Commentary in English*

**Author:** Jaimini  
**Tradition:** Pūrva Mīmāṃsā  
**Primary use:** Dharma, Vedic authority, interpretation, verbal authority, ritual action, sentence meaning, Mīmāṃsā methodology.

Use this as an independent tradition before making detailed Nyāya-Mīmāṃsā comparisons.

---

# 5. Bhartṛhari and Philosophy of Language

## *Vākyapadīya of Bhartṛhari — Kāṇḍa I*

**Author:** Bhartṛhari  
**Tradition:** Vyākaraṇa / philosophy of language  
**Primary use:** *Śabda*, language, cognition, linguistic philosophy.

---

## *The Vākyapadīya of Bhartṛhari — Kāṇḍa II*

**Primary use:** Sentence, word, meaning, linguistic cognition and related philosophical questions.

---

## *The Vākyapadīya of Bhartṛhari — Kāṇḍa III, Part II*

**Primary use:** Advanced linguistic and philosophical analysis.

These texts should be studied as a distinct philosophical framework rather than merely as an extension of Nyāya.

# 7. Source-Use Hierarchy

When researching a question inside this repository, prefer:

```text
Relevant primary text
        ↓
Its supplied commentary / translation
        ↓
Other repository texts
        ↓
Comparative sources
        ↓
General background
```

Do not use a later commentator to overwrite what an earlier primary text actually says.

---

# 8. Citation Practice

For personal research, record:

```text
Author:
Work:
Volume:
Khaṇḍa / Kāṇḍa / Book:
Chapter / section:
Page:
Passage:
Topic:
Notes:
```

When exact bibliographic information cannot be verified from the available edition, do not invent it.

---

# 9. Bibliographic Expansion

Additional works may be added later, but they should be clearly marked as:

- **Primary repository source**
- **Secondary source**
- **Modern comparative source**
- **Reference source**

This prevents accidental mixing of textual authority levels.

---

# Core Principle

> **A bibliography is useful when it tells you not only what you own, but what philosophical work each text can legitimately support.**
