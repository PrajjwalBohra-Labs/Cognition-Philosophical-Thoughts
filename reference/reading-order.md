# Reading Order

This file gives the recommended order for using the repository's books.

It is a **pedagogical sequence**, not a claim about historical chronology.

---

# 1. Stage One — Orientation

## Read

### 1. *Tarkasaṅgraha*

Focus on:

- basic terminology;
- padārthas;
- cognition;
- pramāṇa;
- inference;
- basic ontology.

## Goal

Build a conceptual vocabulary.

Do not attempt to master every technical distinction yet.

---

# 2. Stage Two — Classical Nyāya Framework

## Read

### 2. *Nyāya Sūtras of Gautama*

Begin with foundational material concerning:

- knowledge;
- pramāṇas;
- inference;
- debate;
- argument;
- error;
- self;
- liberation.

Use the four volumes as the primary Nyāya source base.

## Goal

Understand the structure of the system in its classical formulation.

---

# 3. Stage Three — Vaiśeṣika Ontology

## Read

### 3. *Vaiśeṣika Sūtras of Kaṇāda*

Focus on:

- substances;
- qualities;
- action;
- universals;
- particularity;
- inherence;
- absence;
- self;
- mind;
- time;
- space;
- causation.

## Goal

Understand the ontology that later interacts closely with Nyāya.

---

# 4. Stage Four — Broad Nyāya Analysis

## Read

### 4. *Nyāyamañjarī*

Now return to Nyāya at a broader argumentative level.

Focus on:

- rival positions;
- objections;
- replies;
- epistemology;
- metaphysics;
- reasoning.

## Goal

Move from system recognition to philosophical argument.

---

# 5. Stage Five — Advanced Perception

## Read

### 5. *Tattvacintāmaṇi — Pratyakṣa-khaṇḍa*

Study slowly.

Focus on:

- definitions;
- perceptual cognition;
- conditions;
- relations;
- objections;
- technical terminology.

## Goal

Learn how Navya-Nyāya turns a basic concept into a highly precise analytical problem.

---

# 6. Stage Six — Advanced Inference

## Read

### 6. *Tattvacintāmaṇi — Anumāna-khaṇḍa*

This is one of the most demanding stages.

Focus on:

```text
Pakṣa
Sādhya
Hetu
Vyāpti
Anumiti
Hetvābhāsa
```

Then examine definitions and objections at increasing technical depth.

## Goal

Develop rigorous inferential analysis.

---

# 7. Stage Seven — Upamāna

## Read

### 7. *Tattvacintāmaṇi — Upamāna-khaṇḍa*

Focus on:

- comparison;
- similarity;
- cognition;
- whether comparison is reducible to another pramāṇa.

## Goal

Understand pramāṇa classification through a concrete philosophical disagreement.

---

# 8. Stage Eight — Śabda

## Read

### 8. Tattvacintāmaṇi Śabda / Āloka material

Focus on:

- testimony;
- reliable speaker;
- word;
- sentence;
- verbal cognition;
- authority.

## Goal

Prepare for comparison with Mīmāṃsā and Bhartṛhari.

---

# 9. Stage Nine — Mīmāṃsā

## Read

### 9. *Pūrva Mīmāṃsā Sūtra*

Study independently.

Focus on:

- dharma;
- Vedic authority;
- ritual action;
- sentence meaning;
- verbal authority;
- interpretation.

## Rule

Do not read Mīmāṃsā merely as "the opponent of Nyāya."

First understand its own philosophical problem.

---

# 10. Stage Ten — Bhartṛhari

## Read

### 10. *Vākyapadīya — Kāṇḍa I*

Then:

### 11. *Vākyapadīya — Kāṇḍa II*

Then:

### 12. *Vākyapadīya — Kāṇḍa III*

Focus on:

- śabda;
- sentence;
- word;
- meaning;
- sphoṭa;
- cognition.

## Goal

Develop an independent philosophy-of-language framework.

---

# 11. Stage Eleven — Comparative Return

After completing the main sequence, return to:

- Nyāya on *śabda*;
- Mīmāṃsā on language;
- Bhartṛhari on *śabda*;
- Nyāya on cognition;
- Vaiśeṣika on ontology.

Now comparisons will be substantially more meaningful.

---

# 12. Stage Twelve — Navya-Nyāya Re-reading

Return to:

- *Tattvacintāmaṇi — Pratyakṣa*
- *Tattvacintāmaṇi — Anumāna*
- *Tattvacintāmaṇi — Upamāna*
- *Tattvacintāmaṇi — Śabda*

At this stage focus less on "what is the doctrine?" and more on:

```text
What exactly is being defined?
Why is the definition inadequate?
What counterexample is introduced?
How is the definition repaired?
What relation is being specified?
```

---

# 13. Recommended Parallel References

During the whole sequence keep available:

```text
Tarkasaṅgraha
Nyāya Sūtras
Vaiśeṣika Sūtras
Nyāyamañjarī
Glossary
Terminology
Concept map
```

Do not repeatedly read all books simultaneously. Use them as targeted references.

---

# 14. Reading Method

For each serious session:

```text
Read passage
 ↓
Identify terminology
 ↓
Identify question
 ↓
Reconstruct argument
 ↓
Mark objections
 ↓
Record unresolved issues
 ↓
Continue
```

---

# 15. First Pass vs Second Pass

## First pass

Goal:

> Understand the architecture.

Do not get trapped in every technical dispute.

## Second pass

Goal:

> Understand the arguments.

Return to difficult sections.

## Third pass

Goal:

> Compare positions.

Read related passages across schools.

## Fourth pass

Goal:

> Conduct independent analysis.

Formulate your own research questions.

---

# 16. When to Move Forward

Move forward when you can:

- explain the main concepts;
- reconstruct the central arguments;
- distinguish the main neighboring concepts;
- identify major objections;
- explain why the next text requires the previous one.

Do not require perfect mastery before progressing.

---

# 17. When to Go Back

Return to earlier material when:

- a technical term is repeatedly unclear;
- an argument cannot be reconstructed;
- a later text exposes a contradiction in your understanding;
- different schools seem identical when the texts say otherwise;
- you can memorize definitions but cannot apply them.

---

# Core Principle

> **Read in an order that builds dependencies, then reread in an order that reveals disagreements.**
