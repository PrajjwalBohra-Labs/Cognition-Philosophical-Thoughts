# Philosophical Schools

This file introduces the major traditions directly represented in the repository and explains how they should be approached.

The purpose is orientation, not a complete history of Indian philosophy.

---

# 1. Nyāya

## Central concerns

- knowledge;
- pramāṇas;
- inference;
- debate;
- reasoning;
- error;
- language;
- self;
- metaphysics;
- liberation.

## Major repository texts

- *Nyāya Sūtras*
- *Nyāyamañjarī*
- *Tarkasaṅgraha*
- *Tattvacintāmaṇi*

## Characteristic strength

Rigorous analysis of justification, inference, objections, and philosophical argument.

---

# 2. Vaiśeṣika

## Central concerns

- ontology;
- categories;
- substance;
- qualities;
- action;
- universals;
- particulars;
- inherence;
- absence;
- causation;
- self;
- mind;
- time;
- space.

## Major repository text

- *Vaiśeṣika Sūtras*

## Characteristic strength

Systematic categorization of reality and its relations.

---

# 3. Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika

Later intellectual developments bring Nyāya epistemology and Vaiśeṣika ontology into close interaction.

A useful schematic representation is:

```text
Nyāya
Knowledge + inference + argument
          +
Vaiśeṣika
Ontology + categories
          ↓
Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika synthesis
```

Do not assume this synthesis erases differences between the original traditions.

---

# 4. Navya-Nyāya

## Central concern

Technical precision in philosophical analysis.

## Principal repository text

- *Tattvacintāmaṇi*

## Characteristic features

- precise definitions;
- technical relations;
- qualifiers;
- limiting conditions;
- analysis of cognition;
- inferential structure;
- detailed objection and reply.

## Study requirement

Do not begin here without a strong foundation in classical Nyāya concepts.

---

# 5. Pūrva Mīmāṃsā

## Central concerns

- dharma;
- Vedic authority;
- ritual action;
- interpretation;
- verbal authority;
- sentence meaning;
- epistemology.

## Principal repository text

- *Pūrva Mīmāṃsā Sūtra*

## Comparative importance

Mīmāṃsā is essential for understanding disagreements concerning:

- *śabda*;
- Veda;
- sentence meaning;
- authority;
- interpretation;
- knowledge.

---

# 6. Vyākaraṇa / Bhartṛhari's Philosophy of Language

The repository's *Vākyapadīya* provides access to a philosophical treatment of:

- śabda;
- sentence;
- word;
- meaning;
- cognition;
- *sphoṭa*.

This should not simply be classified as another version of Nyāya philosophy of language.

---

# 7. Comparative Map

| School | Primary focus | Key questions |
|---|---|---|
| Nyāya | Epistemology and reasoning | How do we know? How do arguments work? |
| Vaiśeṣika | Ontology | What exists and how is reality categorized? |
| Navya-Nyāya | Technical analysis | How can philosophical distinctions be made maximally precise? |
| Mīmāṃsā | Dharma and language | How is dharma known? How does authoritative language work? |
| Bhartṛhari / Vyākaraṇa | Language and cognition | What is śabda? How does linguistic meaning arise? |

---

# 8. How to Compare Schools

Use:

```text
Problem
 ↓
School A's framing
 ↓
School A's argument
 ↓
School B's framing
 ↓
School B's argument
 ↓
Shared assumptions
 ↓
Different assumptions
 ↓
Consequences
```

Never compare only conclusions.

---

# 9. Common Comparative Mistakes

Avoid:

### Mistake 1

> "All Indian philosophies believe the same thing."

### Mistake 2

> "Nyāya is just Indian logic."

### Mistake 3

> "Mīmāṃsā is only ritualism."

### Mistake 4

> "Vaiśeṣika is just ancient physics."

### Mistake 5

> "Bhartṛhari's sphoṭa is exactly modern semantics."

### Mistake 6

> "Navya-Nyāya is merely complicated Sanskrit."

These descriptions erase the philosophical problems each tradition is actually addressing.

---

# 10. Repository Scope

The collection is strongest in:

- Nyāya;
- Vaiśeṣika;
- Navya-Nyāya;
- Mīmāṃsā;
- Bhartṛhari / philosophy of language.

It is therefore not a complete representation of all Indian philosophical traditions.

Other traditions such as Buddhist, Jain, Sāṃkhya, Yoga, Vedānta, and Cārvāka should not be treated as if they are comprehensively represented by this repository unless additional primary materials are added.

---

# Core Principle

> **A school is best understood through the problems it formulates, the arguments it gives, and the objections it answers.**
