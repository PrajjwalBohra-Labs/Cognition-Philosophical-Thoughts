# Terminology

This is the working terminology reference for the repository.

Definitions are intentionally concise. For technical study, always return to the relevant primary text because Sanskrit philosophical terms can change meaning across schools and contexts.

---

# A

### Abhāva

Absence or non-existence; an important ontological and epistemological category in Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika.

### Adṛṣṭa

"Unseen" or imperceptible factor invoked in certain causal and karmic explanations.

### Anubhava

Presentative or immediate cognition in relevant classifications.

### Anumāna

Inference.

### Anumiti

Inferential cognition or the cognition produced by inference.

### Ātman

Self.

### Āpta

Trustworthy or authoritative person, especially in Nyāya accounts of testimony.

---

# B

### Buddhi

Intellect/cognition; meaning varies by philosophical context.

---

# D

### Darśana

Philosophical viewpoint/system; literally associated with seeing or vision.

### Dravya

Substance.

### Dṛṣṭānta

Example or illustrative instance used in reasoning.

### Dveṣa

Aversion.

### Dharma

A highly context-dependent term; in the repository it can refer to duty, normative order, ritual efficacy, or a specific causal/ethical category depending on school.

---

# G

### Guṇa

Quality or attribute.

---

# H

### Hetu

Reason, probans, or inferential mark.

### Hetvābhāsa

Defective or fallacious reason.

---

# I

### Icchā

Desire.

---

# J

### Jalpa

Adversarial disputation.

### Jāti

A type of futile or inappropriate rejoinder in debate.

### Jñāna

Cognition / knowledge.

---

# K

### Karma

Context-sensitive term.

In Vaiśeṣika ontology it means action/motion as a category.

In broader Indian philosophy it can refer to action and its consequences.

Never translate it mechanically without context.

---

# M

### Manas

Mind/internal organ in Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika.

It should not simply be equated with the modern brain.

### Mithyājñāna

Erroneous cognition.

---

# N

### Nigamana

Conclusion, especially the concluding member of a five-member Nyāya inference.

### Nigrahasthāna

Ground or point of defeat in debate.

### Nirṇaya

Determination or settled judgment.

---

# P

### Padārtha

Category / object of philosophical analysis.

### Pakṣa

Subject or locus under consideration in inference.

### Pramā

Valid cognition.

### Pramāṇa

Means/source of valid cognition.

### Prāmāṇya

Validity or epistemic authoritativeness.

### Prameya

Object of valid cognition.

### Pratijñā

Proposition or thesis.

### Pratyakṣa

Perception.

### Prayojana

Purpose or motive relevant to philosophical inquiry.

---

# S

### Saṃśaya

Doubt.

### Saṃyoga

Conjunction/contact.

### Sādhya

Property to be established by inference.

### Sapakṣa

Similar case/instance in which the relevant property is present.

### Śabda

Word, verbal expression, or testimony; exact technical meaning depends on context.

### Śabdapramāṇa

Verbal testimony as a means of valid cognition.

### Samavāya

Inherence.

### Sāmānya

Universal/general property.

### Sphoṭa

Technical linguistic concept associated especially with Bhartṛhari and grammatical philosophy.

### Smṛti

Memory/recollection.

---

# T

### Tarka

Reasoning used to expose the consequences of a supposition; its exact status differs from an independent pramāṇa.

---

# U

### Upamāna

Comparison; Nyāya treats it as a distinct pramāṇa.

### Upanaya

Application of the general relation to the subject in the five-member Nyāya argument.

---

# V

### Vāda

Philosophical discussion oriented toward determination.

### Vākya

Sentence/utterance.

### Vibhāga

Disjunction/separation.

### Vipakṣa

Dissimilar case where the relevant property is absent.

### Vitaṇḍā

Destructive criticism without necessarily advancing an independent thesis.

### Vyāpti

Invariable concomitance or pervasive inferential relation.

# 6. Translation Rule

When a term has no exact English equivalent:

```text
Sanskrit term
+
transliteration
+
contextual definition
```

is preferable to a misleading one-word translation.

---

# Core Principle

> **Technical vocabulary is part of the argument. Translating a term badly can change the philosophy.**
