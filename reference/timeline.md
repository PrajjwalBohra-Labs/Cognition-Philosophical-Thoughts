# Timeline

This is a **broad intellectual orientation**, not a precise chronology.

Dating Indian philosophical texts is often complex, disputed, and dependent on textual layers, commentarial traditions, and scholarly reconstruction. Dates below should therefore be treated as approximate historical orientation rather than exact publication dates.

---

# 1. Broad Historical Development

```text
Early Vedic intellectual traditions
        ↓
Śramaṇa and classical philosophical developments
        ↓
Early systematic schools
        ↓
Nyāya and Vaiśeṣika
        ↓
Mīmāṃsā and other classical systems
        ↓
Mature inter-school debate
        ↓
Navya-Nyāya technical analysis
        ↓
Early modern scholastic traditions
        ↓
Modern scholarship and comparative philosophy
```

This diagram is intentionally schematic.

---

# 2. Early Foundations

Indian philosophical traditions develop from multiple intellectual environments rather than from one linear philosophical school.

Important broad developments include:

- Vedic traditions;
- Upaniṣadic inquiry;
- Śramaṇa traditions;
- debates concerning self, knowledge, action, liberation, and reality.

The repository itself does not comprehensively cover all of these stages.

---

# 3. Nyāya and Vaiśeṣika

The foundational Nyāya and Vaiśeṣika traditions establish two especially important analytical orientations:

```text
Nyāya
→ knowledge, reasoning, inference, debate

Vaiśeṣika
→ ontology, categories, substances, relations
```

Later developments bring these traditions into increasingly close interaction.

---

# 4. Mīmāṃsā

Pūrva Mīmāṃsā develops an extensive philosophical treatment of:

- dharma;
- Vedic authority;
- ritual action;
- interpretation;
- language;
- epistemology.

It becomes a major participant in debates with Nyāya and other traditions.

---

# 5. Bhartṛhari

Bhartṛhari represents a major development in the philosophical treatment of language.

The *Vākyapadīya* investigates:

- śabda;
- sentence;
- word;
- meaning;
- cognition;
- linguistic unity;
- sphoṭa.

His work should be placed within the intellectual history of Vyākaraṇa rather than treated simply as a branch of Nyāya.

---

# 6. Jayanta Bhaṭṭa

Jayanta Bhaṭṭa represents a mature phase of Nyāya philosophical debate.

His *Nyāyamañjarī* is especially useful for observing:

```text
Nyāya position
 ↓
Rival position
 ↓
Objection
 ↓
Nyāya response
```

This makes the work valuable for comparative study.

---

# 7. Gaṅgeśa and Navya-Nyāya

Gaṅgeśa Upādhyāya is associated with a major transformation in Nyāya analytical technique.

The *Tattvacintāmaṇi* represents a move toward:

- technical definitions;
- precise relational analysis;
- detailed epistemology;
- rigorous treatment of inference;
- highly specialized philosophical terminology.

This period is crucial for understanding Navya-Nyāya.

---

# 8. Later Developments

After Gaṅgeśa, Navya-Nyāya develops into an extensive technical tradition with applications across different philosophical disciplines.

The repository's Tattvacintāmaṇi materials should therefore be understood as part of a much larger analytical tradition rather than as an isolated book.

# 10. Chronology vs Reading Order

Do not confuse:

```text
Historical chronology
```

with:

```text
Pedagogical reading order
```

For this repository, the recommended learning order is:

```text
Tarkasaṅgraha
 ↓
Nyāya Sūtras
 ↓
Vaiśeṣika Sūtras
 ↓
Nyāyamañjarī
 ↓
Tattvacintāmaṇi
 ↓
Mīmāṃsā
 ↓
Vākyapadīya
```

This is chosen for conceptual dependency, not because the texts were historically written in this exact order.

---

# 11. Dating Discipline

When recording a date for a philosopher or text, distinguish:

```text
Traditional attribution
Approximate historical date
Textual dating
Commentarial dating
Modern scholarly dating
```

If a date is disputed, record the uncertainty instead of choosing a falsely precise year.
